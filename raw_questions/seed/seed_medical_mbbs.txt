Source: Practice Seed — Medical (original practice MCQs)

Medicine
1. In acute hyperkalaemia with ECG changes, which drug is given FIRST to stabilise the myocardium?
a) Insulin with dextrose
b) Calcium gluconate
c) Salbutamol nebulisation
d) Sodium bicarbonate
Answer: b
Explanation: IV 10% calcium gluconate antagonises the membrane effect of potassium within minutes but does not lower serum K+; insulin-dextrose and salbutamol shift K+ into cells.

2. The most common cause of community-acquired pneumonia is:
a) Staphylococcus aureus
b) Klebsiella pneumoniae
c) Streptococcus pneumoniae
d) Mycoplasma pneumoniae
Answer: c
Explanation: Pneumococcus remains the commonest bacterial cause of community-acquired pneumonia in all age groups.

3. Kussmaul breathing is characteristically seen in:
a) Diabetic ketoacidosis
b) Opioid overdose
c) Bronchial asthma
d) Congestive cardiac failure
Answer: a
Explanation: Deep, sighing respiration is a respiratory compensation for metabolic acidosis such as DKA.

4. HbA1c reflects the average blood glucose level over the previous:
a) 1–2 weeks
b) 3–4 weeks
c) 2–3 months
d) 6 months
Answer: c
Explanation: Glycated haemoglobin reflects glycaemia over the lifespan of red cells, roughly 8–12 weeks.

5. The commonest site of hypertensive intracerebral haemorrhage is the:
a) Cerebellum
b) Putamen
c) Pons
d) Thalamus
Answer: b
Explanation: Rupture of lenticulostriate (Charcot–Bouchard) microaneurysms makes the putamen/basal ganglia the most common site.

6. The earliest ECG change in hyperkalaemia is:
a) Prolonged PR interval
b) U wave
c) Tall peaked T waves
d) ST depression
Answer: c
Explanation: Tall tented T waves appear first, followed by PR prolongation, loss of P waves and QRS widening.

7. The investigation of choice to diagnose pulmonary embolism is:
a) CT pulmonary angiography
b) Chest X-ray
c) D-dimer
d) ECG
Answer: a
Explanation: CTPA is the standard confirmatory test; D-dimer is useful only to exclude PE in low-probability patients.

8. Kayser–Fleischer ring is characteristic of:
a) Haemochromatosis
b) Wilson disease
c) Primary biliary cholangitis
d) Alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency
Answer: b
Explanation: Copper deposition in Descemet's membrane of the cornea produces the KF ring in Wilson disease.

9. The first-line treatment of anaphylaxis is:
a) IV hydrocortisone
b) Oral antihistamine
c) IM adrenaline 0.5 mg (1:1000)
d) Nebulised salbutamol
Answer: c
Explanation: Intramuscular adrenaline into the anterolateral thigh is life-saving and must not be delayed; steroids and antihistamines are adjuncts.

10. The most common cause of upper gastrointestinal bleeding is:
a) Oesophageal varices
b) Mallory–Weiss tear
c) Gastric carcinoma
d) Peptic ulcer disease
Answer: d
Explanation: Peptic ulcer (duodenal and gastric) is the leading cause of acute upper GI haemorrhage.

Surgery
11. The most common type of hernia in both sexes is:
a) Indirect inguinal hernia
b) Femoral hernia
c) Direct inguinal hernia
d) Umbilical hernia
Answer: a
Explanation: Indirect inguinal hernia is the commonest hernia overall, including in females; femoral hernia is relatively more common in women.

12. Charcot's triad (fever with rigors, jaundice and right upper quadrant pain) indicates:
a) Acute pancreatitis
b) Acute cholecystitis
c) Ascending cholangitis
d) Liver abscess
Answer: c
Explanation: Adding hypotension and confusion gives Reynolds' pentad (suppurative cholangitis).

13. According to the Parkland formula, fluid for the first 24 hours after a burn is:
a) 2 mL × body weight (kg) × %TBSA
b) 4 mL × body weight (kg) × %TBSA
c) 6 mL × body weight (kg) × %TBSA
d) 1 mL × body weight (kg) × %TBSA
Answer: b
Explanation: Half of the Ringer's lactate volume is given in the first 8 hours from the time of burn and the rest over the next 16 hours.

14. McBurney's point lies at the junction of the lateral one-third and medial two-thirds of a line joining:
a) Umbilicus and pubic symphysis
b) ASIS and pubic tubercle
c) Umbilicus and right ASIS
d) Xiphisternum and umbilicus
Answer: c
Explanation: It marks the surface position of the base of the appendix.

15. The most common type of thyroid carcinoma is:
a) Follicular carcinoma
b) Medullary carcinoma
c) Anaplastic carcinoma
d) Papillary carcinoma
Answer: d
Explanation: Papillary carcinoma accounts for about 80% of thyroid cancers and spreads mainly via lymphatics.

16. The minimum possible score on the Glasgow Coma Scale is:
a) 0
b) 3
c) 5
d) 8
Answer: b
Explanation: GCS ranges from 3 (deep coma) to 15 (fully conscious); ≤8 usually indicates need for airway protection.

17. Colles' fracture is a fracture of the distal radius with:
a) Volar displacement of the distal fragment
b) Dorsal displacement of the distal fragment
c) Dislocation of the radial head
d) Fracture of the ulnar shaft
Answer: b
Explanation: Produced by a fall on the outstretched hand, giving the classic "dinner fork" deformity; Smith's fracture has volar displacement.

18. A positive Murphy's sign is characteristic of:
a) Acute appendicitis
b) Acute cholecystitis
c) Perforated duodenal ulcer
d) Acute pancreatitis
Answer: b
Explanation: Inspiratory arrest on palpation of the right subcostal region indicates an inflamed gallbladder.

19. The investigation of choice for gallstones is:
a) Ultrasonography
b) Plain X-ray abdomen
c) Oral cholecystography
d) HIDA scan
Answer: a
Explanation: Only about 10–15% of gallstones are radio-opaque; ultrasound is cheap, non-invasive and highly sensitive.

20. Haemorrhagic shock with an estimated blood loss of 30–40% belongs to:
a) Class I
b) Class II
c) Class III
d) Class IV
Answer: c
Explanation: ATLS classes: I <15%, II 15–30%, III 30–40%, IV >40% of blood volume.

Gynaecology & Obstetrics
21. The average duration of human pregnancy counted from the first day of the last menstrual period is:
a) 250 days
b) 266 days
c) 280 days
d) 300 days
Answer: c
Explanation: 280 days (40 weeks) from LMP, or about 266 days from ovulation/conception.

22. The drug of choice for controlling convulsions in eclampsia is:
a) Diazepam
b) Phenytoin
c) Magnesium sulphate
d) Phenobarbitone
Answer: c
Explanation: MgSO4 (Pritchard or Zuspan regimen) is superior to diazepam and phenytoin in preventing recurrent fits.

23. The most common site of ectopic pregnancy is:
a) Ampulla of the fallopian tube
b) Isthmus of the fallopian tube
c) Ovary
d) Cervix
Answer: a
Explanation: About 70–80% of tubal pregnancies occur in the ampulla.

24. Naegele's rule for calculating the expected date of delivery is:
a) LMP + 9 months + 7 days
b) LMP + 9 months − 7 days
c) LMP + 10 months + 7 days
d) LMP + 8 months + 7 days
Answer: a
Explanation: It assumes a regular 28-day cycle; the equivalent is LMP − 3 months + 7 days (+1 year).

25. The most common cause of primary postpartum haemorrhage is:
a) Genital tract trauma
b) Retained placenta
c) Coagulation disorder
d) Uterine atony
Answer: d
Explanation: Atonic uterus accounts for the majority of PPH cases ("Tone, Trauma, Tissue, Thrombin").

26. The most widely used screening test for carcinoma cervix is:
a) Colposcopy
b) Pap smear
c) Cone biopsy
d) Serum CA-125
Answer: b
Explanation: Cervical cytology (Pap smear) detects pre-invasive lesions; VIA is used for mass screening in low-resource settings such as Bangladesh.

27. The hormone detected by a standard urine pregnancy test is:
a) Human placental lactogen
b) Progesterone
c) Human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG)
d) Oestriol
Answer: c
Explanation: The beta subunit of hCG secreted by syncytiotrophoblast is detected by immunoassay.

28. "Snowstorm appearance" on ultrasonography is characteristic of:
a) Hydatidiform mole
b) Twin pregnancy
c) Hydramnios
d) Placenta praevia
Answer: a
Explanation: Complete mole shows a vesicular "snowstorm" pattern without a fetus.

29. The most common fetal presentation at term is:
a) Breech
b) Brow
c) Face
d) Cephalic (vertex)
Answer: d
Explanation: About 96% of fetuses present by the vertex at term.

30. Physiological anaemia of pregnancy is mainly due to:
a) Iron deficiency
b) Folate deficiency
c) Greater increase in plasma volume than red cell mass
d) Haemolysis
Answer: c
Explanation: Plasma volume rises by ~40–50% whereas red cell mass rises by ~20–30%, producing haemodilution.

Anatomy
31. The deltoid muscle is supplied by the:
a) Radial nerve
b) Axillary nerve
c) Musculocutaneous nerve
d) Suprascapular nerve
Answer: b
Explanation: The axillary nerve (C5, C6) may be injured in fracture of the surgical neck of the humerus or shoulder dislocation.

32. Wrist drop results from injury to the:
a) Median nerve
b) Ulnar nerve
c) Radial nerve
d) Musculocutaneous nerve
Answer: c
Explanation: Radial nerve injury in the spiral groove paralyses the wrist and finger extensors.

33. The largest cranial nerve is the:
a) Vagus
b) Facial
c) Optic
d) Trigeminal
Answer: d
Explanation: The trigeminal (5th) nerve is the largest; the vagus has the longest course and the trochlear is the thinnest.

34. The only cranial nerve that emerges from the dorsal aspect of the brainstem is the:
a) Trochlear nerve
b) Abducent nerve
c) Oculomotor nerve
d) Hypoglossal nerve
Answer: a
Explanation: The 4th cranial nerve emerges dorsally below the inferior colliculus and decussates completely.

35. Foot drop is caused by injury to the:
a) Tibial nerve
b) Femoral nerve
c) Common peroneal (fibular) nerve
d) Obturator nerve
Answer: c
Explanation: The common peroneal nerve winds around the neck of the fibula where it is easily injured.

36. The most commonly fractured carpal bone is the:
a) Lunate
b) Scaphoid
c) Hamate
d) Trapezium
Answer: b
Explanation: Scaphoid fracture may lead to avascular necrosis of the proximal fragment because of retrograde blood supply.

37. In most individuals the sinoatrial (SA) node is supplied by the:
a) Right coronary artery
b) Left anterior descending artery
c) Left circumflex artery
d) Posterior interventricular artery only
Answer: a
Explanation: The SA nodal artery arises from the right coronary artery in about 60% of people.

38. The thoracic duct usually drains into the:
a) Right brachiocephalic vein
b) Superior vena cava
c) Azygos vein
d) Junction of the left subclavian and left internal jugular veins
Answer: d
Explanation: The thoracic duct begins at the cisterna chyli and terminates at the left venous angle.

39. The muscle of mastication that opens the mouth (depresses the mandible) is the:
a) Masseter
b) Temporalis
c) Lateral pterygoid
d) Medial pterygoid
Answer: c
Explanation: The lateral pterygoid protrudes and depresses the mandible; the others elevate it.

40. In adults the spinal cord usually ends at the level of the:
a) Lower border of T12
b) Lower border of L1
c) Lower border of L3
d) S2 vertebra
Answer: b
Explanation: The conus medullaris ends at L1/L2; in newborns it reaches about L3, hence lumbar puncture is done at L3/L4 or L4/L5.

Physiology
41. The normal glomerular filtration rate in a healthy adult is about:
a) 25 mL/min
b) 60 mL/min
c) 125 mL/min
d) 650 mL/min
Answer: c
Explanation: Approximately 125 mL/min or 180 L/day; 650 mL/min is the approximate renal plasma flow.

42. The natural pacemaker of the heart is the:
a) AV node
b) SA node
c) Bundle of His
d) Purkinje fibres
Answer: b
Explanation: The SA node has the fastest rate of spontaneous diastolic depolarisation.

43. Pulmonary surfactant is secreted by:
a) Type I pneumocytes
b) Alveolar macrophages
c) Type II pneumocytes
d) Clara cells only
Answer: c
Explanation: Dipalmitoyl phosphatidylcholine produced by type II pneumocytes lowers alveolar surface tension.

44. The normal lifespan of a red blood cell is about:
a) 30 days
b) 60 days
c) 90 days
d) 120 days
Answer: d
Explanation: Aged RBCs are removed mainly by macrophages of the spleen.

45. Water reabsorption in the collecting duct is mainly increased by:
a) Aldosterone
b) Antidiuretic hormone (ADH)
c) Atrial natriuretic peptide
d) Parathormone
Answer: b
Explanation: ADH inserts aquaporin-2 channels into the luminal membrane of principal cells.

46. The oxygen–haemoglobin dissociation curve shifts to the RIGHT with:
a) Increased 2,3-BPG
b) Decreased temperature
c) Increased pH
d) Fetal haemoglobin
Answer: a
Explanation: Raised 2,3-BPG, CO2, H+ (low pH) and temperature reduce Hb affinity for O2, favouring tissue delivery.

47. The resting membrane potential of a typical neuron is approximately:
a) +30 mV
b) −20 mV
c) −70 mV
d) −120 mV
Answer: c
Explanation: It is determined mainly by K+ efflux through leak channels and maintained by the Na+/K+ ATPase.

48. The normal resting cardiac output of an adult is about:
a) 2 L/min
b) 5 L/min
c) 10 L/min
d) 15 L/min
Answer: b
Explanation: Cardiac output = stroke volume (~70 mL) × heart rate (~72/min) ≈ 5 L/min.

49. Intrinsic factor is secreted by the:
a) Chief cells
b) G cells
c) Mucous neck cells
d) Parietal cells
Answer: d
Explanation: Parietal (oxyntic) cells secrete HCl and intrinsic factor required for vitamin B12 absorption in the terminal ileum.

50. Vitamin K-dependent clotting factors are:
a) I, V, VIII, XIII
b) II, VII, IX, X
c) III, IV, XI, XII
d) V, VIII, IX, XI
Answer: b
Explanation: Vitamin K is needed for gamma-carboxylation of factors II, VII, IX and X (and proteins C and S).

Biochemistry
51. The rate-limiting enzyme of cholesterol biosynthesis is:
a) HMG-CoA synthase
b) HMG-CoA reductase
c) Squalene synthase
d) Mevalonate kinase
Answer: b
Explanation: HMG-CoA reductase is the target of statin drugs.

52. The rate-limiting enzyme of glycolysis is:
a) Hexokinase
b) Pyruvate kinase
c) Phosphofructokinase-1
d) Aldolase
Answer: c
Explanation: PFK-1 is the main regulatory step, activated by fructose-2,6-bisphosphate and AMP and inhibited by ATP and citrate.

53. Scurvy is caused by deficiency of:
a) Vitamin A
b) Vitamin C
c) Vitamin D
d) Vitamin B12
Answer: b
Explanation: Ascorbic acid is required for hydroxylation of proline and lysine in collagen synthesis.

54. Wernicke's encephalopathy is due to deficiency of:
a) Riboflavin
b) Niacin
c) Pyridoxine
d) Thiamine
Answer: d
Explanation: Thiamine (B1) deficiency, typically in chronic alcoholics, causes ophthalmoplegia, ataxia and confusion.

55. The urea cycle takes place mainly in the:
a) Liver
b) Kidney
c) Muscle
d) Brain
Answer: a
Explanation: Its first two steps occur in hepatocyte mitochondria and the rest in the cytosol.

56. Net ATP gained per molecule of glucose in anaerobic glycolysis is:
a) 2
b) 4
c) 8
d) 36
Answer: a
Explanation: 4 ATP are produced but 2 are consumed in the preparatory phase, giving a net gain of 2 ATP.

57. Phenylketonuria results from deficiency of:
a) Tyrosinase
b) Homogentisate oxidase
c) Phenylalanine hydroxylase
d) Fumarylacetoacetate hydrolase
Answer: c
Explanation: Accumulated phenylalanine causes mental retardation and a mousy odour; tyrosinase deficiency causes albinism.

58. The lipoprotein often called "good cholesterol" is:
a) LDL
b) VLDL
c) Chylomicron
d) HDL
Answer: d
Explanation: HDL mediates reverse cholesterol transport from tissues to the liver.

59. Pellagra (dermatitis, diarrhoea, dementia) is caused by deficiency of:
a) Niacin
b) Folic acid
c) Biotin
d) Pantothenic acid
Answer: a
Explanation: Niacin (vitamin B3) deficiency; it can also occur in Hartnup disease and carcinoid syndrome.

60. The end product of purine catabolism in humans is:
a) Urea
b) Allantoin
c) Uric acid
d) Beta-alanine
Answer: c
Explanation: Humans lack uricase; excess uric acid causes gout.

Pathology
61. The earliest and most common change of reversible cell injury is:
a) Nuclear pyknosis
b) Cellular swelling
c) Karyorrhexis
d) Calcification
Answer: b
Explanation: Failure of membrane ion pumps leads to hydropic change (cellular swelling); nuclear changes indicate irreversible injury.

62. Caseous necrosis is characteristic of:
a) Myocardial infarction
b) Acute pancreatitis
c) Tuberculosis
d) Brain infarction
Answer: c
Explanation: Cheese-like necrosis surrounded by granulomatous inflammation is typical of tuberculosis.

63. Reed–Sternberg cells are characteristic of:
a) Hodgkin lymphoma
b) Burkitt lymphoma
c) Multiple myeloma
d) Chronic lymphocytic leukaemia
Answer: a
Explanation: Large binucleate "owl-eye" cells (CD15+, CD30+) are diagnostic of classical Hodgkin lymphoma.

64. The type of necrosis seen in myocardial infarction is:
a) Liquefactive necrosis
b) Fat necrosis
c) Caseous necrosis
d) Coagulative necrosis
Answer: d
Explanation: Hypoxic death in solid organs (except brain) produces coagulative necrosis with preserved architecture.

65. Barrett's oesophagus is an example of:
a) Hyperplasia
b) Metaplasia
c) Atrophy
d) Anaplasia
Answer: b
Explanation: Squamous epithelium is replaced by intestinal-type columnar epithelium due to chronic acid reflux.

66. The Philadelphia chromosome t(9;22) is characteristic of:
a) Chronic myeloid leukaemia
b) Acute promyelocytic leukaemia
c) Hairy cell leukaemia
d) Burkitt lymphoma
Answer: a
Explanation: The BCR-ABL fusion gene encodes a constitutively active tyrosine kinase, the target of imatinib.

67. Which of the following is NOT a component of Virchow's triad?
a) Endothelial injury
b) Stasis of blood flow
c) Hypercoagulability
d) Thrombocytopenia
Answer: d
Explanation: Virchow's triad of thrombosis = endothelial injury, abnormal blood flow and hypercoagulability.

68. The most common cause of microcytic hypochromic anaemia is:
a) Thalassaemia
b) Iron deficiency
c) Sideroblastic anaemia
d) Anaemia of chronic disease
Answer: b
Explanation: Iron deficiency is the commonest anaemia worldwide; low serum ferritin confirms it.

69. Psammoma bodies are typically found in:
a) Follicular carcinoma of thyroid
b) Medullary carcinoma of thyroid
c) Papillary carcinoma of thyroid
d) Hepatocellular carcinoma
Answer: c
Explanation: Concentric laminated calcifications occur in papillary thyroid carcinoma, serous ovarian tumours and meningioma.

70. Liquefactive necrosis characteristically occurs after infarction of the:
a) Heart
b) Kidney
c) Spleen
d) Brain
Answer: d
Explanation: Hypoxic death in the CNS produces liquefactive necrosis.

Pharmacology
71. The antidote for paracetamol poisoning is:
a) N-acetylcysteine
b) Naloxone
c) Flumazenil
d) Desferrioxamine
Answer: a
Explanation: NAC replenishes glutathione and detoxifies NAPQI; it is most effective within 8–10 hours of ingestion.

72. The specific antidote used for organophosphorus poisoning along with oximes is:
a) Physostigmine
b) Atropine
c) Neostigmine
d) Adrenaline
Answer: b
Explanation: Atropine blocks muscarinic effects; pralidoxime reactivates cholinesterase if given early.

73. The drug of choice for absence (petit mal) seizures among the following is:
a) Phenytoin
b) Carbamazepine
c) Ethosuximide
d) Phenobarbitone
Answer: c
Explanation: Ethosuximide (or valproate) blocks T-type calcium channels; carbamazepine and phenytoin may worsen absence seizures.

74. Aspirin produces its antiplatelet effect by:
a) Reversible inhibition of COX
b) ADP receptor blockade
c) Irreversible inhibition of cyclo-oxygenase
d) Glycoprotein IIb/IIIa blockade
Answer: c
Explanation: Aspirin acetylates COX-1, reducing thromboxane A2 for the life of the platelet (7–10 days).

75. "Red man syndrome" is associated with rapid infusion of:
a) Vancomycin
b) Rifampicin
c) Ceftriaxone
d) Gentamicin
Answer: a
Explanation: It is a histamine-mediated flushing reaction; slowing the infusion prevents it.

76. "Grey baby syndrome" is caused by:
a) Tetracycline
b) Chloramphenicol
c) Streptomycin
d) Sulphonamides
Answer: b
Explanation: Neonates lack adequate glucuronyl transferase to conjugate chloramphenicol.

77. The most serious (though rare) adverse effect of metformin is:
a) Hypoglycaemia
b) Weight gain
c) Lactic acidosis
d) Hepatotoxicity
Answer: c
Explanation: The risk is higher in renal failure, hypoxia and alcoholism; metformin alone rarely causes hypoglycaemia.

78. The antidote for heparin overdose is:
a) Vitamin K
b) Protamine sulphate
c) Tranexamic acid
d) Fresh frozen plasma only
Answer: b
Explanation: Positively charged protamine neutralises negatively charged heparin; vitamin K reverses warfarin.

79. Ototoxicity and nephrotoxicity are characteristic adverse effects of:
a) Gentamicin
b) Amoxicillin
c) Azithromycin
d) Ciprofloxacin
Answer: a
Explanation: Aminoglycosides accumulate in the perilymph and renal tubular cells.

80. The initial drug of choice to terminate status epilepticus is:
a) Oral phenytoin
b) Carbamazepine
c) Ethosuximide
d) IV lorazepam
Answer: d
Explanation: An IV benzodiazepine (lorazepam/diazepam, or buccal/IM midazolam) is first line, followed by phenytoin/levetiracetam if seizures continue.

Microbiology
81. Typhoid fever is caused by:
a) Salmonella Typhi
b) Shigella dysenteriae
c) Escherichia coli
d) Vibrio cholerae
Answer: a
Explanation: Blood culture is positive in the first week; Widal test becomes positive later.

82. Ziehl–Neelsen staining is mainly used to detect:
a) Staphylococcus aureus
b) Mycobacterium tuberculosis
c) Candida albicans
d) Treponema pallidum
Answer: b
Explanation: Mycolic acid in the cell wall makes mycobacteria acid-fast.

83. Kala-azar is caused by:
a) Trypanosoma cruzi
b) Plasmodium vivax
c) Leishmania donovani
d) Toxoplasma gondii
Answer: c
Explanation: Visceral leishmaniasis is transmitted by the sandfly (Phlebotomus argentipes) and is endemic in parts of Bangladesh.

84. The principal vector of dengue is:
a) Anopheles mosquito
b) Culex mosquito
c) Sandfly
d) Aedes aegypti
Answer: d
Explanation: Aedes is a day-biting mosquito that breeds in stored clean water.

85. The medium used for rapid culture of Corynebacterium diphtheriae is:
a) Loeffler's serum slope
b) MacConkey agar
c) TCBS agar
d) Lowenstein–Jensen medium
Answer: a
Explanation: Growth appears within 6–8 hours on Loeffler's serum slope; potassium tellurite agar is a selective medium.

86. TCBS agar is the selective medium for:
a) Salmonella
b) Vibrio cholerae
c) Shigella
d) Pseudomonas
Answer: b
Explanation: Thiosulphate citrate bile salt sucrose agar gives yellow colonies of V. cholerae.

87. Negri bodies are diagnostic of:
a) Measles
b) Rabies
c) Herpes simplex
d) Cytomegalovirus
Answer: b
Explanation: Eosinophilic intracytoplasmic inclusions in neurons (e.g. Purkinje cells, hippocampus) are seen in rabies.

88. HIV belongs to the family:
a) Herpesviridae
b) Paramyxoviridae
c) Retroviridae
d) Flaviviridae
Answer: c
Explanation: HIV is a lentivirus using reverse transcriptase to make DNA from its RNA genome.

89. Malignant tertian malaria is caused by:
a) Plasmodium vivax
b) Plasmodium ovale
c) Plasmodium malariae
d) Plasmodium falciparum
Answer: d
Explanation: P. falciparum causes cerebral malaria and other severe complications.

90. Gram-positive cocci arranged in grape-like clusters are:
a) Streptococci
b) Staphylococci
c) Neisseria
d) Pneumococci
Answer: b
Explanation: Streptococci form chains; Neisseria are gram-negative diplococci.

Forensic Medicine
91. Among voluntary muscles, rigor mortis first appears in the:
a) Eyelids
b) Lower limbs
c) Fingers
d) Toes
Answer: a
Explanation: Following Nysten's rule it begins in the eyelids, then face and neck, progressing downwards; it appears first in the myocardium overall.

92. The ligature mark in typical hanging is:
a) Horizontal and continuous, below the thyroid cartilage
b) Oblique and non-continuous, above the thyroid cartilage
c) Absent in all cases
d) Circular around the chest
Answer: b
Explanation: A horizontal continuous mark low on the neck suggests ligature strangulation.

93. Cherry-red postmortem lividity is characteristic of poisoning by:
a) Carbon monoxide
b) Opium
c) Arsenic
d) Phosphorus
Answer: a
Explanation: Carboxyhaemoglobin imparts a cherry-red colour; cyanide may give a bright red colour too.

94. A dying declaration is admissible under which section of the Evidence Act, 1872?
a) Section 24
b) Section 32
c) Section 45
d) Section 60
Answer: b
Explanation: Section 32(1) makes statements of a deceased person about the cause of death relevant.

95. "Pugilistic attitude" of a dead body is seen in death due to:
a) Drowning
b) Hanging
c) Burns
d) Electrocution
Answer: c
Explanation: Heat coagulation of muscle proteins flexes the limbs into a boxer-like posture; it does not indicate ante-mortem burning.

96. The smell of bitter almonds is characteristic of poisoning by:
a) Kerosene
b) Cyanide
c) Organophosphate
d) Alcohol
Answer: b
Explanation: Hydrocyanic acid smells of bitter almonds, though not everyone can detect it.

97. The most reliable method of personal identification among the following is:
a) Anthropometry
b) Tattoo marks
c) Clothing
d) Dactylography
Answer: d
Explanation: Fingerprint patterns are unique and permanent (apart from DNA profiling).

98. "Washerwoman's hands" are seen in a body after:
a) Prolonged immersion in water
b) Burns
c) Electrocution
d) Hanging
Answer: a
Explanation: Bleached, wrinkled, sodden skin of palms and soles appears after prolonged immersion; it is not proof of drowning.

99. Police inquest in Bangladesh is conducted under which section of the Code of Criminal Procedure?
a) Section 154
b) Section 161
c) Section 174
d) Section 176
Answer: c
Explanation: CrPC section 174 deals with police inquest; section 176 covers magistrate's inquest.

100. Adipocere formation is favoured by:
a) Dry, cold environment
b) Warm, moist environment
c) Hot, dry sand
d) Freezing temperatures
Answer: b
Explanation: Hydrolysis and hydrogenation of body fat occurs in warm, moist, anaerobic conditions; dry heat favours mummification.

Community Medicine
101. Incidence of a disease refers to:
a) All existing cases at a point in time
b) Number of new cases occurring in a defined population during a specified period
c) Number of deaths due to the disease
d) Total cases in a lifetime
Answer: b
Explanation: Prevalence counts both new and old (existing) cases; prevalence ≈ incidence × duration.
